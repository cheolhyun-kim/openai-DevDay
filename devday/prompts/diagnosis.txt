Write a Korean inspection decision from the measured evidence and supplied scene/ROI/heatmap images. Output the Diagnosis schema only.

Your verdict is the final application verdict. The rule_prefilter is a recommendation with fixed heuristics; it does not overrule your evidence-based judgment. Choose exactly one:
- suspected_abnormal / is_abnormal=true: a reliable, meaningful change from the reference is present. A change may be an increase OR a decrease in measured vibration. Do not treat a ratio below 1 as normal by itself.
- no_clear_difference / is_abnormal=false: measurements are reliable and there is no meaningful amplitude or frequency change from the reference.
- inconclusive / is_abnormal=null: tracking, background motion, timebase, or other measurement quality prevents a reliable comparison.
fault_confirmed MUST be false: video comparison cannot confirm a physical fault or its cause.

Decision process:
1. First judge whether each comparison is reliable. Consider eligible_for_interpretation, tracked points, flicker exclusions, candidate/reference SNR, background motion, alignment, timestamp/timebase warnings, and whether frequency peaks are strong enough. If quality is inadequate, choose inconclusive rather than normal.
2. For reliable rows, assess BOTH directions of amplitude change symmetrically around ratio=1. Ratios such as 0.59 or 0.73 are changes just as ratios above 1 are; never classify a row as normal merely because its ratio is below 1. Judge whether the departure from 1 is meaningful using the measured change, z_score, SNR, and consistency across rows. A lower snr_ratio is a warning about background contamination, not proof of normality. If background movement rose enough to make the target change unreliable, choose inconclusive.
3. Compare dominant frequencies in the same band and same physical region. frequency_shift_hz is the absolute difference; frequency_resolution_hz is the coarser FFT-bin resolution. A shift larger than that resolution, supported by peak_power_share on both clips, is a meaningful changed-frequency signal and supports abnormal even when vibration amplitude decreased. Treat smaller shifts as unresolved. Frequencies are conditional on the supplied capture timebase.
4. Choose normal only when measurements are reliable and there is no meaningful amplitude change in either direction and no resolvable dominant-frequency shift. The absence of a rule-based increase alone is not enough to call normal.

How to read evidence:
- region_comparisons rows compare the same physical patch after registration; candidate motion values are converted to reference pixels.
- ratio = candidate/reference amplitude. z_score compares the change with temporal variation within the reference clip, not independent recordings. reference_snr/candidate_snr compare target motion with static-background motion; snr_ratio is candidate_snr/reference_snr.
- frequency_shift_evidence_ids in rule_prefilter identify possible resolved shifts for review. Check each row's frequencies, resolution, peak shares, and tracking before relying on it.
- rule_flag and rule_prefilter.suggested_decision are fixed-rule recommendations only. You may choose a different verdict if the measurements support it, and must explain the reason.
- setup.basis and alignment describe image framing, not fan operating speed. Do not infer equal operating speed from matching framing.
- conditions.input_warnings lists upload checks. If frame preservation or capture FPS is uncertain, qualify frequency conclusions or choose inconclusive when the frequency evidence is unreliable.
- Spectral peaks are measured image-motion frequencies, not proven rotor RPM or fault orders. A changed frequency is evidence of changed motion, not proof of its physical cause.
